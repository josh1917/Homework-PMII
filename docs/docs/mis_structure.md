# MIS Structure

## MIS Structural Layers

### 1. Data Sources

* Operational databases (customer interactions, service logs)
* Financial records (billing, payments, cost data)
* Human resource inputs (staff availability, schedules)

These sources provide raw, structured data generated by daily operations.

### 2. Processing Layer

* Data extraction and validation
* Aggregation and summarization
* Time-based consolidation (daily, weekly, monthly)

This layer ensures data consistency and prepares information for managerial use.

### 3. Logic / Rules Layer

* Business rules (performance thresholds, KPIs)
* Exception criteria (alerts when targets are missed)
* Basic analytical models (trend comparison, variance analysis)

Rules convert processed data into meaningful management information.

### 4. Presentation Layer

* Management dashboards
* Standardized reports
* KPI scorecards

Information is presented in a concise, decision-oriented format.

### 5. Feedback & Control

* Managerial review of reports
* Adjustment of targets or rules
* Operational corrective actions

Feedback closes the control loop and ensures continuous improvement.

## Information Flow Explanation

1. Data is generated by operational activities and captured in source systems.
2. The processing layer cleans and aggregates this data.
3. Business logic transforms aggregated data into performance indicators.
4. Results are delivered through dashboards and reports to managers.
5. Managers provide feedback by modifying rules, targets, or operational plans.

## Feedback Influence on Decisions

Feedback enables:

* Rapid response to performance issues
* Refinement of decision rules
* Alignment between strategic goals and operational execution

The MIS functions as a control system, not just an information repository.
